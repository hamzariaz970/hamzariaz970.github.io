Under review2026NLP · Knowledge distillation

Bridging Syntax and Semantics: Distilling LLMs into Symbolic Rules for Few-Shot Relation Extraction

Hamza Riaz and Mihai Surdeanu

ACL Rolling Review, October 2026 cycle. Planned commitment to NAACL 2027.

Distilling an LLM’s knowledge through offline paraphrase generation into reusable symbolic rules, then classifying new sentences with a dependency parser and deterministic matching.

Read manuscript ↗
How the method works +

A multi-pass sieve starts with exact dependency-path matches and progressively relaxes syntactic constraints when stricter sieves find no match. Entity-type and lemma checks retain semantic information, and a Jaccard filter rejects weak candidates. The rules can be reused without calling an LLM for each prediction.

Under review2026Multimodal learning · Clinical AI

Accuracy Is Not Safety: Ordinal, Calibrated Multimodal Prediction of KTAS Triage Levels

Saim Kaleem*, Hamza Riaz*, Muhammad Naseer Bajwa, and Muhammad Moazam Fraz

Artificial Intelligence in Medicine * Equal contribution

Combining written complaints and clinical measurements for emergency triage, with class weighting, ordinal training objectives, calibration assessment, and selective prediction.

Read manuscript ↗Code ↗
What I worked on +

I co-developed a model that projects complaint text and clinical measurements separately before fusion. The work examines how training objectives account for class imbalance and the ordering of urgency levels, with attention to calibration and performance on critical cases.

Hamza Riaz, Arham Haroon, Maha Baig, Muhammad Dawood Rizwan, Muhammad Naseer Bajwa, and Muhammad Moazam Fraz

International Conference on Robotics and Automation in Industry (ICRAI), IEEE

A four-agent chest X-ray reporting system that uses current-image evidence and knowledge graphs to support diagnostic statements, with retrieved reports guiding wording and structure.

03 / Background

A little about my path

Full CV ↗ (PDF, opens in a new tab)

My path into computer science was not straightforward. After completing my A-Levels, I could not decide between medicine and engineering. In 2021, I received an offer from a medical college in Pakistan and visited it. But something felt off. The next day, I rejected the offer and took a gap year. I could not save one year only to spend a lifetime regretting my choice.

Computer science was never really an option I had considered. I used to joke that coding made my head spin. But my love for technology, especially consumer gadgets and PC hardware, made me want to give it a chance. I prepared for admission to NUST, a university I had never imagined myself studying at.

My earlier interest in medicine stayed with me. My research on medical triage and radiology reporting led me to questions about the reliability of AI systems and the evidence behind their decisions. As LLM agents began to emerge, I was fascinated by what they could do. Their progress has been impressive, but they can still be expensive to use, and their decisions are not always reliable.

At the CLU Lab, I worked on distilling a language model’s knowledge into reusable symbolic rules, so new examples could be classified without calling the model each time. I want to keep working on making language models and agents more reliable and efficient, so more people can afford to use and benefit from them. I also want to find better ways to study their internal knowledge and understand how it shapes their behavior. These are questions I hope to pursue during my PhD.

In my free time, I love playing video games and I’m a huge Chelsea football fan.
